## The Home and Garden Boom on Shopify

Home and garden is one of the fastest-growing categories on Shopify, driven by the sustained interest in home improvement that accelerated during the pandemic and has not slowed down. From furniture and decor to outdoor living and gardening tools, DTC brands are carving out profitable niches in a market long dominated by big-box retailers.

We tore down 30 top home and garden Shopify stores to understand how they structure their businesses, price their products, and compete for customers.

## Store Profile: What Top Brands Look Like

The median home and garden store in our analysis carries **112 products** organized into **16 collections**. Catalogs tend to be broader than beauty or food brands but narrower than fashion, reflecting the physical complexity and higher price points of home goods.

Average product price across the category: **$68**. This places home and garden in the upper tier of Shopify pricing, behind only electronics and luxury goods.

## Pricing Architecture: Three Distinct Tiers

Nearly every successful home and garden store uses a clear pricing architecture with three tiers:

### Entry Tier ($15 to $40)

Small accessories, decorative items, and consumable products. These items serve as entry points for new customers and impulse additions to larger orders. Examples include candles, small planters, kitchen utensils, and decorative accents.

### Core Tier ($40 to $120)

The bread and butter of home and garden stores. Mid-range products that balance quality perception with accessible pricing. Examples include throw pillows, medium planters, garden tools, lighting fixtures, and organizational products.

### Premium Tier ($120 to $500+)

Furniture pieces, large outdoor items, and premium collections. These high-ticket items drive the majority of revenue for stores that carry them. Customers often discover a brand through entry or core tier products and graduate to premium purchases over time.

## Catalog Strategy: Rooms and Occasions

Home and garden brands organize collections differently from other categories. Rather than organizing purely by product type, the best stores create collections around **rooms** (living room, bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, outdoor) and **occasions** (holiday entertaining, spring refresh, new home essentials).

This organization mirrors how customers actually shop. Someone redecorating their living room wants to see all living room products together — furniture, decor, lighting, and textiles — not browse separate categories for each product type.

Among our 30 stores, those using room-based or occasion-based collections had **34% more collections on average** than those using only product-type organization.

## Seasonal Dynamics: The Spring and Fall Surges

Home and garden is one of the most seasonal categories on Shopify. Our **ShopiSpy** tracking data shows two distinct activity peaks:

- **Spring (March to May):** Outdoor and garden product launches, spring cleaning collections, fresh color palettes
- **Fall (September to November):** Holiday decor, cozy home collections, entertaining essentials, gifting sets

During these peaks, top stores increase their new product additions by **40 to 60%** compared to off-peak months. They also create seasonal collections that are promoted heavily and then archived after the season ends.

Stores that plan their catalog around these seasonal cycles — rather than maintaining a static year-round assortment — consistently show more catalog activity and pricing dynamism.

## Photography and Visual Merchandising

Home and garden stores invest more in product photography than almost any other Shopify category. The standard is lifestyle photography showing products in styled room settings, not just white-background product shots.

Common patterns among top performers:

- **Room scene photography** for every major product
- **Scale reference images** showing products in context with furniture and people
- **Detail shots** highlighting materials, textures, and craftsmanship
- **Seasonal restyling** of the same products in different settings for different times of year

This investment in visual content directly supports higher price points. Customers are more willing to pay $85 for a throw pillow when they can see it styled beautifully in a living room context.

## Pricing Behavior: Stable with Strategic Promotions

Unlike electronics or fashion, home and garden pricing is relatively stable. Only **28% of stores changed product prices** during our 90-day tracking window, compared to the all-category average of 38%. When prices do change, it is typically for one of two reasons:

- **Seasonal clearance:** End-of-season markdowns on outdoor or holiday-specific items
- **New collection launches:** Slight adjustments to existing products when new arrivals shift the pricing landscape

Home and garden brands prefer to run promotional events (sitewide percentage discounts, free shipping thresholds) rather than adjusting individual product prices. This protects perceived brand value while still offering purchase incentives.

## The Gifting Opportunity

**54% of stores in our analysis** had a dedicated gift collection or gift-specific products (gift cards, curated gift boxes, gift bundles). Home and garden items are natural gifts — decorative, practical, and universally appealing.

Stores that capitalize on gifting tend to:

- Create curated gift guides organized by price point or recipient
- Offer gift wrapping as a paid add-on service
- Launch gift-specific bundles around major holidays
- Price gift bundles at common gift-giving thresholds ($50, $75, $100)

## What Sets the Best Apart

After analyzing 30 stores through **ShopiSpy**, the differentiators for top home and garden brands are clear:

1. **Room-based and occasion-based collections** that match how customers shop
2. **Clear three-tier pricing** that gives customers an entry point and an upgrade path
3. **Seasonal catalog management** with planned product launches and collection rotations
4. **Investment in lifestyle photography** that justifies premium pricing
5. **Gifting infrastructure** that captures high-intent seasonal buyers
6. **Stable base pricing** with strategic sitewide promotions rather than constant markdowns

## The Competitive Landscape Ahead

Home and garden on Shopify is still less saturated than fashion or beauty, which means there is room for new brands to establish themselves. But the bar for quality — in products, photography, and customer experience — is rising. Brands entering this space need strong visual merchandising, a clear pricing strategy, and the operational capacity to manage seasonal catalog cycles. Those that get it right are building some of the most profitable DTC businesses on Shopify today.
