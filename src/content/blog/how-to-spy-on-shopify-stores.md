## Why Spy on Shopify Stores?

Whether you are launching a new ecommerce brand or trying to stay ahead of established competitors, understanding what other Shopify stores are doing is one of the smartest moves you can make. Competitor intelligence helps you identify trending products, benchmark your pricing, and spot gaps in the market before anyone else does.

The good news is that you do not need an expensive subscription to get started. Below are seven free methods you can use right now to analyze any Shopify store.

## Method 1: View the Product Catalog via /products.json

Every Shopify store exposes a machine-readable product feed at its `/products.json` endpoint. Simply append `/products.json` to the store's URL and you will see a JSON file containing product titles, descriptions, prices, images, variants, and creation dates.

**What you can learn:**

- Full product titles and descriptions
- All variant pricing and inventory status
- Product creation and update timestamps
- Image URLs and alt text

This is the single most powerful free method because it gives you structured data you can analyze in a spreadsheet or script. Some stores disable this endpoint, but the majority still leave it accessible.

## Method 2: Check the Page Source for Theme Information

Right-clicking any Shopify store and selecting "View Page Source" reveals a wealth of technical information. Look for the `Shopify.theme` object in the HTML to identify the exact theme and version the store uses.

**Pro tip:** Search for `theme_store_id` in the source code. If this value is present, the store is using a theme from the Shopify Theme Store, and you can look it up by ID to find the exact theme name and developer.

## Method 3: Use BuiltWith or Wappalyzer for Tech Stack Analysis

Free browser extensions like **BuiltWith** and **Wappalyzer** detect the technology stack behind any website. When you visit a Shopify store with one of these installed, you will see which apps, analytics tools, payment gateways, and marketing platforms the store uses.

**Key things to look for:**

- Email marketing provider (Klaviyo, Mailchimp, Omnisend)
- Review apps (Judge.me, Loox, Stamped)
- Upsell and cross-sell tools
- Analytics and tracking scripts

Understanding a competitor's tech stack tells you what strategies they are investing in.

## Method 4: Browse the Wayback Machine for Historical Data

The **Internet Archive's Wayback Machine** (web.archive.org) captures historical snapshots of websites. Enter a competitor's URL and you can see how their store has evolved over time, including past product lineups, pricing changes, homepage designs, and promotional campaigns.

This is especially useful for understanding seasonal pricing patterns. If a competitor runs the same sale every Black Friday, you can plan your own promotions accordingly.

## Method 5: Monitor Social Media Ad Libraries

Both Meta and TikTok maintain **public ad libraries** where you can search for any brand's active advertisements. This gives you insight into:

- Which products competitors are promoting most heavily
- The creative formats and copy angles they are testing
- How long specific ads have been running (longer = likely profitable)
- Target audience signals based on ad placement

Search for a competitor's brand name or domain in the Meta Ad Library or TikTok Creative Center to see everything they are running.

## Method 6: Track Pricing with ShopiSpy's Free Tier

While manual methods work, they do not scale. **ShopiSpy** offers a free tier that lets you track a limited number of competitor stores and receive alerts when products or prices change. This automates the most tedious part of competitor research and ensures you never miss a significant move.

With ShopiSpy, you can set up monitoring in under two minutes and start receiving notifications via email the same day. It is one of the fastest ways to go from zero to actionable competitor intelligence.

## Method 7: Analyze Traffic with SimilarWeb's Free Version

**SimilarWeb** provides free traffic estimates for any website. While the free version has limitations, it still reveals:

- Estimated monthly visits
- Traffic sources breakdown (organic, paid, social, direct)
- Top referring websites
- Geographic distribution of visitors

Combine this traffic data with the product and pricing intel from the other methods, and you have a comprehensive picture of how a competitor's store operates.

## Putting It All Together

Each of these methods reveals a different piece of the puzzle. The most effective approach is to combine them:

1. **Start with /products.json** to understand the product catalog and pricing
2. **Check the tech stack** to see what tools power the store
3. **Review ad libraries** to understand their marketing strategy
4. **Set up ShopiSpy alerts** to monitor changes automatically going forward
5. **Use SimilarWeb** for traffic context

### A Sample Competitive Analysis Workflow

Here is a practical workflow you can follow for any competitor:

- **Day 1:** Export their product catalog and log baseline prices
- **Day 2:** Document their tech stack, review apps, and email capture strategy
- **Day 3:** Analyze their active ads and identify their hero products
- **Day 4:** Set up automated monitoring with ShopiSpy so you catch future changes
- **Day 5:** Compile findings into a brief and share with your team

## What to Do With Your Intelligence

Gathering data is only valuable if you act on it. Here are three high-impact actions you can take immediately:

- **Adjust your pricing** if you discover you are significantly more expensive on comparable products
- **Fill product gaps** by offering items your competitors do not carry
- **Improve your tech stack** by adopting proven apps your competitors use successfully

Competitor intelligence is not about copying. It is about making better-informed decisions for your own business. The seven methods above give you everything you need to start — no budget required.
